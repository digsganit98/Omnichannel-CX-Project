# BFSI Knowledge Base — Regulatory FAQs

These cover common questions about rules and protections that apply across banks and insurers in India. For specifics about your account, use the mobile banking app, the net-banking portal, message us on WhatsApp, or call the 24/7 helpline at 1800-200-1947.

## Deposit insurance

Q: Are my bank deposits insured?
A: Yes. Bank deposits in India are insured by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC), a subsidiary of the RBI, up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank. This cover includes the principal and interest together and applies across all your accounts at the same bank combined. If you hold deposits at more than one bank, each bank is covered separately.

## Nomination

Q: What is nomination and why is it important?
A: Nomination lets you name a person who will receive the funds or benefits from your account, deposit, locker, or insurance policy in the event of your death. It makes settlement faster and easier for your family. You can add or change a nominee at any time through the app, net-banking, or by submitting a request. It is strongly recommended to keep your nomination up to date.

## Free-look / cooling-off for insurance

Q: Can I cancel an insurance policy after buying it?
A: Yes. Insurance policies come with a free-look period, typically around 15 to 30 days from receiving the policy document, during which you can review the terms and cancel if it does not suit you. On cancellation within this period, the premium is refunded after deducting certain charges such as stamp duty and proportionate risk cover. This is a customer protection under IRDAI rules.

## KYC and re-KYC

Q: Why am I asked to update my KYC periodically?
A: RBI requires banks to keep customer information current through periodic KYC updation (re-KYC). How often depends on your risk category. Keeping KYC updated ensures uninterrupted access to your accounts and services. You may be able to complete re-KYC through the app or net-banking, or by submitting fresh identity and address proof.

## Inactive and dormant accounts

Q: What happens if I do not use my account for a long time?
A: If there is no customer-initiated activity for about two years, an account is classified as inactive and then dormant, as per RBI norms. A dormant account has restricted operations for your protection. You can reactivate it by completing KYC and making a transaction. Deposits that remain unclaimed for ten years are transferred to the RBI's Depositor Education and Awareness (DEA) Fund, but you can still claim them at any time through the bank.

## Customer rights and data privacy

Q: What rights do I have as a customer?
A: You are entitled to fair and transparent treatment, clear disclosure of charges and terms, protection of your personal data, and access to grievance redressal if something goes wrong. Your personal information is handled with consent and used for the purposes you agreed to. If you have a complaint, you can raise it through customer support and escalate it through the grievance redressal process if needed.
